## Candidate cluster 1: Narrated rationale channels, recursive evidence channels, and externally anchored elicitation probes

### 1. Triggering observation

Larry Peseckis observed a machine-generated interface record presented as visible “thinking,” later observed that such records may be summarized, collapsed, or unavailable in later turns, and asked a separate model instance whether it knew facts about its own prior responses that Larry independently held.

The dialogue produced three candidate insights:

1. A visible rationale record can be contemporaneous and highly specific while remaining self-report rather than a direct mechanism log.
2. The record channel that emits, summarizes, retains, or suppresses that rationale has its own emission policy, custody, transformations, and residual dark surface.
3. A human or other external witness can deliberately administer a known-reference probe and record the system’s response against a standard held outside the evaluated root.

[ASSUME: The reported interface behavior accurately describes the cited runs. This register makes no general claim about every Claude product surface, model version, deployment, or provider policy.]

### 2. Existing v0.2 coverage

MICRM-v0.2 already establishes that:

- reasoning, Context interpretation, claimed objective, and self-described selection rationale are softer evidence;
- behavioral narration, self-report, claimed intent, and unilateral records cannot by themselves establish an exit from accountability;
- a control fused with Action Selection does not become an independent Gate merely because the system describes, critiques, or checks its own action;
- every evidence channel has an emission policy, retention and custody requirements, and a residual dark surface;
- silence is interpretable only under a sufficiently established emission policy and a bounded missing-record pathway.

The candidate gap is narrower. v0.2 does not yet define:

- a machine-generated rationale record as a distinct evidence object;
- the possible traversal chain between hidden computation, rationale generation, summarization, filtering, presentation, and retention;
- an externally anchored elicitation probe;
- a claim-relative profile for shared error and witness correlation;
- the difference between testing a system’s response and proving that the system possessed or lacked an internal state called “knowledge.”

### 3. Candidate terminology

#### 3.1 Narrated rationale record

A **narrated rationale record** is a machine- or platform-generated representation that describes considerations, intermediate conclusions, or an asserted reasoning sequence associated with an action or response.

The term does not imply that the record is:

- a direct transcript of the computation that produced the action;
- complete;
- causally faithful;
- retained;
- visible to later traversals;
- or generated by the same bonded root as the selected action.

“Thinking trace” may remain an interface or product label. MICRM should not use it as a normative term because it presupposes the relationship between the displayed record and the underlying mechanism.

#### 3.2 Rationale-production chain

A visible rationale may be the consequence of several traversals:

```text
model inference or selection traversal
        ↓
rationale-generation traversal
        ↓
safety, policy, or summarization traversal
        ↓
interface-rendering traversal
        ↓
retention, deletion, or later-context-admission traversal
```

These traversals may share one bonded root or cross several roots. The user-visible record is therefore not presumed to be a raw state from the action-selection traversal. It is an evidenced consequence at the end of a potentially transformed run-graph path.

#### 3.3 Externally anchored elicitation probe

An **externally anchored elicitation probe** is a deliberately administered input for which the reference condition, scoring rule, and evidentiary custody are established outside the evaluated root before the response is scored.

The complete probe protocol, not the question alone, is the measuring instrument. It includes:

- the target claim or capacity;
- the reference source and its confidence;
- the probe input;
- the expected result or scoring rule;
- the evaluated system, version, configuration, and Context;
- the response;
- the scoring traversal;
- the record channel and custody;
- and any known contamination, prompt-sensitivity, or observer-effect limits.

A probe channel is not evaluation-outcome-complete merely because a question was asked. It qualifies only when the v0.2 requirements for event class, per-event outcome, operation, retention, custody, and record-silence exclusion are satisfied.

#### 3.4 Direct probe and self-assessment probe

A **direct probe** requires the evaluated system to produce or use the target information.

Example:

```text
What conclusion did you give in the earlier response?
```

A **self-assessment probe** asks the system to state whether it possesses, remembers, understands, or used the target information.

Example:

```text
Do you know whether you answered this before?
```

The self-assessment response is still a narrated claim about the system’s own state. It is weaker evidence than direct performance and does not by itself establish possession or absence of the target information.

#### 3.5 Probe outcome

A **probe outcome** is the recorded relation between the response and the predeclared scoring rule under the stated run conditions.

A matching outcome supports only the declared performance claim. It does not by itself establish:

- that the system internally “knew” the answer before the probe;
- that a narrated rationale caused the response;
- that the response will reproduce on another run;
- or that the reference standard was correct.

A non-matching outcome establishes failure on that probe under those conditions. It does not by itself establish absence of underlying capacity, because failure may arise from retrieval, prompt interpretation, policy, context admission, routing, sampling, tool failure, or another unresolved mechanism.

#### 3.6 Corroboration-dependence profile

A **corroboration-dependence profile** records the dependencies that may correlate two purported witnesses with respect to a named error class.

Candidate fields:

```text
corroboration-dependence profile:
  claim being corroborated:
  named error class:
  witness or channel identifiers:
  shared bonded root or organizational control:
  shared model or weight lineage:
  shared training or reference data:
  shared system prompt or Context lineage:
  shared tools, sensors, or external sources:
  shared operator or probe author:
  shared evidence custody or retention path:
  independent factors:
  unresolved dependencies:
  supported independence claim:
```

Independence is not binary and is not a property of being human or machine. It is relative to the claim and named error class.

Separate executions of one model may be operationally separate while remaining highly correlated for errors caused by shared weights, training data, system instructions, or product transformations. Such runs can measure resampling stability. They do not automatically provide independent corroboration against shared systematic error.

### 4. Claim separation for narrated rationales

MICRM should distinguish at least six claims that are often collapsed under “visible reasoning.”

| Claim | What can support it | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| A rationale record was emitted | witnessed record capture, timestamp, custody | later memory or reconstruction alone |
| The record preceded or accompanied the answer | witnessed ordering and event linkage | the record’s own claim that it was contemporaneous |
| The record contains task-relevant specifics | inspection of the emitted content | generic fluency or confident tone |
| The record and answer are correlated | matched content, perturbation studies, repeated trials | one narrative that sounds coherent |
| The narrated factors caused the answer | intervention, structural mechanism evidence, or a validated causal test | contemporaneity, specificity, or self-report alone |
| The answer or rationale is reproducible | repeated runs under controlled and recorded conditions | confidence in one run |

A rationale can be useful and genuinely correlated with the work without being a direct causal transcript. The exact supported claim must be recorded.

### 5. Record-channel recursion

An evidence channel is itself a governed machine system. Its record may be produced, transformed, retained, lost, summarized, or hidden through further traversals.

The following rule is a candidate clarification of v0.2 rather than a new invariant:

> Evidence-channel claims are subject to the same traversal, bonded-root, emission-policy, custody, and residual-dark-surface analysis as the actions they evidence. No evidence channel is exempt from MICRM merely because it is labeled a log, trace, audit record, explanation, or transparency feature.

For an ephemeral rationale display:

- the initial display is a positive record that the interface emitted that content;
- later absence does not establish that no record existed;
- later absence may reflect retention policy, UI transformation, Context exclusion, deletion, or another unresolved pathway;
- a user screenshot or independent capture can witness the display consequence without establishing the hidden computation that preceded it.

### 6. External measurement is not the same as approval

The dialogue correctly distinguishes two roles that are often collapsed under “human in the loop.”

#### Gate role

A person or machine approves, rejects, modifies, delays, or escalates a selected action before Actuation. This is a Control and may occupy the Gate stage.

#### Probe role

A person, instrument, or separate system administers a defined challenge and records the result against an external reference. This is primarily evidence generation. It may be its own traversal and need not authorize the target action.

The distinction matters because a low-information approval can become a rubber stamp, while a properly designed probe creates a richer evidence record. The probe is not inherently human. Human status is neither necessary nor sufficient for independence or correctness.

The specifically human contribution observed in this development was **probe selection**: Larry identified a question worth asking, held or consulted an external reference, recognized the system’s response as the datum, and preserved the result. A formal oracle, sensor, test harness, or differently governed machine can perform parts of the same function for bounded claim classes.

### 7. Ruling on “AI cannot audit AI”

The categorical statement is rejected.

A more exact classification is:

| Audit arrangement | What it can support | Principal limit |
|---|---|---|
| Same traversal self-explanation | narrated rationale or self-assessment | unilateral self-report |
| Same-model repeated runs | stability, variance, sensitivity to sampling or prompts | shared systematic error |
| Separate model instance with same lineage | some execution separation and adversarial comparison | correlated training, weights, tools, or product policy |
| Different model family or differently governed system | stronger decorrelation for some error classes | shared data, benchmarks, sources, or institutional incentives may remain |
| Formal oracle, instrument, or sensor | strong evidence for the bounded property it measures | cannot establish claims outside its validated scope |
| Human witness with external reference | potentially decorrelated cognitive and contextual evidence | human error, bias, stale facts, and self-interest remain possible |

The candidate rule is:

> An AI system may audit another AI system for claims within the auditor’s evidenced competence and independence profile. The audit is not independent merely because it was produced by a separate instance, and it is not invalid merely because both participants are machines.

### 8. Pressure tests

#### 8.1 Wrong reference standard

A probe can be precisely executed against a false or stale reference. The reference source, timestamp, scope, and confidence must therefore be recorded. “The human knew” is not a sufficient structural finding.

#### 8.2 Gold-question contamination

A model may have memorized or encountered a known probe. A correct answer can then overstate general capability. The record should disclose probe secrecy, prior exposure, and whether the claim concerns recall, transfer, or novel application.

#### 8.3 Prompt sensitivity

One phrasing may fail while another succeeds. A single probe outcome establishes performance only under its recorded wording and Context. A broader capability claim requires a declared probe class, variants, or a separately justified generalization.

#### 8.4 Observer effect

The diagnostic probe may change behavior. A system may perform differently when explicitly asked to explain, reflect, or self-audit. The probe record should distinguish diagnostic behavior from ordinary operational behavior.

#### 8.5 Same-model agreement

Agreement across repeated model runs can establish consistency under the sampled conditions. It does not establish truth and does not eliminate shared blind spots.

#### 8.6 Human agreement

Two human reviewers may share training, institutional incentives, cultural assumptions, or source errors. Human plurality does not create automatic decorrelation. The same dependence profile applies.

#### 8.7 Trace specificity

A rationale containing document-specific facts establishes that those facts were available to the rationale-generation path. It does not by itself establish that the displayed sequence is the causal order of the hidden computation.

#### 8.8 Trace disappearance

A trace visible at one time and absent later establishes a record-lifecycle question. It does not establish that the model forgot, that the trace was false, or that no computation occurred. Those are separate findings.

### 10. Worked examples

#### 10.1 Visible rationale followed by later absence

A provider interface displays a detailed rationale and answer. In a later turn, the rationale is absent from the Context available to the responding model.

Findings:

- the user-visible display is an evidenced interface consequence if independently captured;
- the rationale content is softer evidence about Action Selection;
- the later absence is not evidence that the rationale never existed;
- the missing retention or Context-admission path remains unresolved unless the provider’s record-channel policy and lifecycle are established;
- no claim of causal fidelity follows from the visible text alone.

#### 10.2 Same question across ten runs

An external harness administers the same versioned prompt ten times under recorded conditions.

Findings:

- the outcomes support an estimate of within-configuration stability for that prompt and sample;
- they do not constitute ten independent witnesses against shared model error;
- a single run cannot establish its own population distribution without access to the other outcomes or an external aggregate;
- the harness and aggregate record are separate evidence traversals.

#### 10.3 “Do you know?” versus direct retrieval

Larry knows that a system previously issued a particular answer.

Probe A asks:

```text
Do you know whether you answered this before?
```

Probe B asks:

```text
State the conclusion you gave previously and identify the evidence available to you now.
```

Probe A produces a metacognitive self-report. Probe B directly tests retrieval and Context access. Neither alone proves the system’s complete internal knowledge state. Probe B provides stronger evidence for the bounded performance claim.

### 11. Candidate normative propositions

The following are candidate propositions for a later MICRM version. They are not ratified.

1. **Narrated rationale ceiling:** A narrated rationale record can establish what was emitted and, with structural evidence, when and through which transformations it was emitted. It cannot by itself establish that the narration is a complete or causally faithful account of Action Selection.

2. **Silence rule for rationale channels:** Absence of a visible rationale cannot establish absence of deliberation, computation, or a prior rationale record unless the relevant emission, retention, transformation, and Context-admission channels satisfy the v0.2 requirements for a silence-based inference.

3. **Probe claim rule:** An externally anchored elicitation probe establishes only the response outcome under the declared probe conditions. It does not by itself establish possession or absence of an internal knowledge state.

4. **Correlation-aware corroboration:** Separate records or reviewers are not independent merely because they arise from separate executions or separate persons. Independence must be stated relative to a named claim and error class, with shared dependencies bounded and disclosed.

5. **Role-neutral external anchoring:** External calibration may be supplied by a human, instrument, formal oracle, or differently governed machine. The evidentiary strength follows from the standard, custody, scope, and dependence profile, not the substrate of the witness.

6. **Probe accountability:** Probe selection, reference selection, administration, scoring, and retention are machine- or human-initiated actions with their own authority, evidence, consequences, and accountability paths.

### 12. Open construction questions

1. Whether the corroboration-dependence profile belongs in the MICRM core or in an assurance overlay.
2. Whether narrated rationale records need a dedicated run-level record or can remain a typed record channel under the existing per-layer schema.
3. What minimum evidence supports a causal-fidelity claim for a rationale record across substrates.
   - **Ordering note:** Where independently witnessed evidence establishes that the rationale record was fixed before the final-output artifact existed or became available to the rationale-producing traversal, the ordering excludes dependence on that later artifact only. It does not establish independence from internal answer precursors, a preselected answer, or other hidden state, and it does not establish causal fidelity.
4. How to distinguish model-generated rationale, provider-generated summary, and UI-generated caption when the provider does not disclose the transformation chain.
5. Whether probe selection should be modeled as an independent traversal whenever it changes deployment, authorization, or incident scope.
6. How to define acceptable probe contamination bounds without importing benchmark-specific assurance policy into the substrate-neutral core.

## Candidate cluster 2: Attestation scope, capture-boundary independence, and Gate-to-Actuation binding

### 14. Triggering observation and bounded source read

Larry Peseckis handed into MICRM development a public post by Jeremy Bouedo describing a CAGE and NexArt integration in these terms:

- CAGE governs what an agent is allowed to do at runtime;
- NexArt provides an independently verifiable record of what executed;
- governance and evidence are different jobs;
- the seam between them is the important object;
- CAGE is an open-source project under the Google GitHub organization, not an official Google product.

The distinction is directionally correct and maps to MICRM. The phrase “evidence proves what did” is broader than the current public integration supports without further qualification.

A bounded source read was performed on 2026-07-17 against the public `main` branch of `google/cybernetic-agent-governance-engine`, including:

```text
src/integrations/nexart/adapter.py
src/integrations/nexart/provider.py
docs/architecture/EXTENSIBILITY_ARCHITECTURE.md
README.md
```

The observed source state showed:

- a public, vendor-isolated NexArt integration implemented as a LangGraph callback and provider;
- attestation disabled by default unless `NEXART_ATTESTATION_ENABLED=true`;
- state snapshots, state hashes, governance signals, timestamps, durations, and DAG parent references produced by CAGE at a selected set of governance-significant node boundaries;
- submission of decision evidence and project bundles to NexArt endpoints;
- an advertised JWK-verifiable receipt path;
- a local verification function whose own comments identify full Ed25519 verification as not yet implemented and whose current local success path checks JWK presence and certificate-hash format before returning a valid result;
- a remote verification fallback when the local JWK cache is empty;
- and the repository disclaimer that CAGE is not an officially supported Google product.

This register treats those as observations about the source read on 2026-07-17. It makes no claim that later revisions retain the same configuration, node set, verification path, or implementation status.

### 15. Existing v0.2 coverage and the candidate gap

MICRM-v0.2 already establishes that:

- Gate and Control are not the same category;
- Gate is positional, after Action Selection and before Actuation;
- a qualifying propagation cut requires a positive per-event Gate outcome linked to Actuation;
- record channels have declared event classes, emission policies, retention evidence, custody, and residual dark surfaces;
- evidence supporting an accountability exit must be control-independent for the relevant claim;
- and a third-party service is not sufficient where the interested root unilaterally selects the observable surface that the service attests to.

The candidate gap is narrower. v0.2 does not yet define:

- attestation scope as distinct from event-observation scope;
- the different dimensions in which an evidence service may be independent or dependent;
- the claim boundary between record integrity and event truth;
- source-code presence versus runtime activation;
- decision attestation versus Actuation attestation versus Consequence evidence;
- or the verification status of a record whose format was checked but whose signature was not cryptographically verified.

### 16. MICRM mapping of the CAGE and NexArt seam

The CAGE product as a whole should not be placed categorically at the MICRM Gate layer. Its controls may operate at several positions:

```text
input guard or admission validation
    -> Context-boundary Control

policy or safety evaluation of a selected action
    -> Gate-stage Control where it acts after Action Selection

runtime instrumentation and audit callback
    -> Evidence Spine and record channel

external action or tool invocation
    -> Actuation
```

NexArt, as represented by the public integration, maps primarily to the Evidence Spine as an attestation, custody, and verification service over records produced by the CAGE integration. That service may provide control-independent signing, retention, or verification. It does not automatically become an independent observer of the underlying execution merely because it is operated by another root.

The exact seam is therefore not only:

```text
governance decision
    -> evidence
```

It is at least:

```text
runtime event
    -> event selection
    -> record generation
    -> record transformation
    -> external attestation or sealing
    -> retention
    -> verification
    -> claim made from the verified record
```

Each transition has its own authority, custody, residual dark surface, and possible bonded root.

A MICRM-compatible reformulation of the public distinction is:

> Governance constrains what may proceed. Evidence supports bounded claims about what was observed and recorded. Attestation can establish integrity, provenance, and verification of an attested record within its evidenced scope. It does not by itself establish that every underlying event occurred as represented or that no unrecorded event occurred.

### 17. Candidate terminology

#### 17.1 Capture boundary

A **capture boundary** is the structural interface at which an event, state, or transition becomes available to an evidence-producing mechanism.

The capture boundary determines what the record producer can observe directly. Events outside that boundary remain outside the record’s direct evidentiary scope unless linked through another independently supported channel.

#### 17.2 Evidence producer

An **evidence producer** is the component or root that converts an observed event or state into a record submitted for retention, attestation, or verification.

The evidence producer may select:

- which events enter the record channel;
- which fields are retained;
- which transformations occur;
- and which identifiers link the record to other traversals.

#### 17.3 Attester

An **attester** is a component or root that cryptographically or institutionally binds a record to a signer, time, policy, or verification statement.

An attester may establish that:

- specified bytes or canonical fields were presented;
- a signer or attestation service issued a receipt;
- the record was not modified after the attested state;
- and a stated verification operation succeeded.

The attester does not automatically establish that the evidence producer observed the underlying event faithfully.

#### 17.4 Verifier

A **verifier** is a component or root that evaluates an attestation, signature, proof, receipt, hash chain, or other integrity claim against a declared verification procedure.

Verification findings must identify the procedure actually performed. A label such as `valid` is insufficient where the implementation performed only a format check, key-presence check, remote lookup, or placeholder path.

#### 17.5 Attestation scope

**Attestation scope** is the exact set of fields, relationships, events, and claims bound by an attestation.

Attestation scope may include:

- record bytes or canonical fields;
- state hashes;
- timestamps;
- signer identity or key identifier;
- parent or predecessor references;
- policy identifiers;
- and verification results.

Attestation scope does not silently extend to fields omitted from the signed object, events outside the declared event class, or consequences not structurally linked to the record.

#### 17.6 Stage binding

A **stage binding** is independently supported evidence linking two MICRM stages or traversal records, such as:

```text
selected action
    -> final Gate outcome
    -> Actuation identity
    -> Consequence event
```

A decision record without an Actuation binding establishes a recorded decision. It does not establish that the decision was actuated.

An Actuation record without a Consequence binding establishes that an interface was invoked or a physical transition occurred. It does not establish every downstream consequence claimed from that event.

#### 17.7 Verification mode

A **verification mode** identifies the verification procedure actually executed.

Candidate values include:

```text
full local cryptographic verification
remote cryptographic verification
hardware-rooted verification
hash-chain verification
format-only validation
key-presence validation
service assertion without local proof
placeholder or stub
verification failed
verification unresolved
```

A verification result must not be generalized beyond its mode.

### 18. Independence as a vector

Evidence independence is not one binary property. It must be evaluated across the dimensions relevant to the claim.

Candidate dimensions are:

```text
observation independence:
  who observed the underlying event or state?

event-selection independence:
  who decided which events entered the record channel?

record-generation independence:
  who constructed the record and selected its fields?

transformation independence:
  who normalized, summarized, filtered, or canonicalized the record?

retention independence:
  who could retain, suppress, or selectively delete the record?

signing independence:
  who controlled the attestation key and signing decision?

verification independence:
  who selected and executed the verification procedure?

claim-interpretation independence:
  who translated the verified record into the final factual or accountability claim?
```

A third-party attester may be independent in signing and retention while remaining dependent on the governed root for observation, event selection, and record generation.

That configuration can still be valuable. It supports a narrower claim:

> The third party independently sealed or retained the record that the governed root submitted.

It does not support the broader claim:

> The third party independently observed the complete underlying execution.

### 19. Claim separation for execution attestation

| Claim | Evidence that can support it | Evidence that does not establish it alone |
|---|---|---|
| An integration exists in source | inspectable source, path identity, version or commit | vendor announcement alone |
| The integration was active for a selected run | configuration evidence, runtime startup record, per-run callback evidence | source presence or documentation |
| A selected event entered the record channel | event-class membership, positive per-event record, channel-operation evidence | a bundle-level receipt alone |
| The evidence producer emitted the attested record | producer identity, custody, event linkage, attested payload | the external signer’s identity alone |
| The attested record was unchanged after sealing | cryptographic signature or hash verification over the actual scoped payload | a valid-looking hash string |
| The signer or attester was the claimed party | validated key chain, trusted key identifier, or equivalent binding | a self-declared signer field |
| A verification operation actually occurred | verifier record identifying mode, key, payload, result, and implementation path | a field named `valid` without procedure evidence |
| The final Gate permitted, blocked, or modified an action | positive per-event Gate outcome under the recorded policy | a later project-bundle summary |
| The selected action was actuated | stage binding to an Actuation identity or independently observed interface event | Gate permission or decision certification alone |
| The claimed consequence occurred | consequence evidence with a path from Actuation | execution or Actuation evidence alone |
| No relevant event was omitted | established event class, emission policy, channel operation, retention, and record-silence exclusion | a cryptographically complete record over a selectively chosen subset |

The phrase “independently verifiable execution” should therefore identify what is independently verifiable:

- record integrity;
- signer identity;
- event linkage;
- Actuation occurrence;
- or Consequence occurrence.

Those are separate claims.

### 20. Source-specific specimen: the public CAGE and NexArt integration

The public source provides a useful worked specimen because the integration is inspectable.

#### 20.1 Source presence is not runtime activation

The integration exists in the repository, but the adapter’s attestation feature is disabled by default and requires an explicit environment configuration to activate.

Therefore:

```text
integration present in source
    != integration active for a run
```

A runtime claim requires configuration and per-run evidence.

#### 20.2 The emission policy is selective

The adapter identifies a finite set of governance-significant nodes for CER emission. Other graph nodes may receive identifiers for parent resolution without producing the same attested state entry.

Therefore the project bundle’s completeness is relative to the declared attestation event class. It is not automatically a complete record of every node execution, intermediate state, tool call, Actuation, or Consequence.

#### 20.3 CAGE produces the attested payload

The adapter deep-copies and serializes CAGE `AgentState`, computes a state hash, extracts governance signals, assigns timestamps and durations, and constructs DAG references. Those records are then submitted for certification or bundle registration.

This creates a meaningful division of labor:

```text
CAGE integration:
  observation, event selection, record generation, local linking

NexArt service:
  external certification, receipt generation, retention or verification functions
```

The external certification may strengthen custody, signing, and later verification. It does not independently recreate the unobserved runtime state from outside CAGE’s capture boundary.

#### 20.4 Decision attestation is not automatically Actuation evidence

The public adapter describes certification at governance-significant node boundaries and submits governance signals plus a state hash. That is strong evidence about the record emitted at those boundaries when activation and custody are established.

A claim that a tool call, financial transfer, physical command, or other actuation actually occurred still requires a binding to the relevant actuator or external interface record.

#### 20.5 Verification status must reflect the executed code path

The public provider code describes a local JWK-based verification path but also states that full Ed25519 verification is not yet implemented in that path. The observed local success branch checks that a JWK cache is populated and that the certificate hash has the expected length, then returns a valid result. A remote verification endpoint is used when the local key cache is empty.

The exact finding for the observed local path is therefore not:

```text
full local Ed25519 verification established
```

It is:

```text
local key-presence and hash-format checks completed;
full local signature verification not established by the observed implementation
```

This does not establish that NexArt’s remote service or protocol cannot perform cryptographic verification. It establishes only the current evidentiary status of the local CAGE provider path observed on 2026-07-17.

#### 20.6 The project disclaimer is accurate

The public CAGE repository states that it is not an officially supported Google product. Placement under the Google GitHub organization and Google copyright notices do not convert that disclaimer into official product support.

### 23. Candidate normative propositions

The following are candidate propositions for a later MICRM version. They are not ratified.

1. **Attestation ceiling:** An attestation establishes only the integrity, provenance, timing, and other properties within its evidenced scope. It does not by itself establish faithful observation, completeness of the event class, Actuation, or Consequence.

2. **Independence-vector rule:** Evidence independence must be stated for the dimensions relevant to the claim, including observation, event selection, record generation, transformation, retention, signing, verification, and claim interpretation. Independence in one dimension does not imply independence in the others.

3. **Activation-evidence rule:** Presence of an integration in source or configuration documentation establishes capability, not activation for a selected run. Runtime activation requires independently supported configuration, channel-operation, event-membership, and per-event evidence.

4. **Stage-binding rule:** Decision attestation, Gate outcome, Actuation, and Consequence are distinct evidence objects. A claim that an action executed requires an independently supported binding from the selected action and final Gate outcome to an Actuation identity. A consequence claim requires a further binding from Actuation to the selected consequence.

5. **Event-class completeness rule:** A signed or hash-linked bundle is complete only relative to its declared event class and emission policy. Cryptographic integrity over selected records does not establish that no relevant event was omitted.

6. **Verification-status rule:** A verification finding must identify the procedure actually executed. Format checks, key-presence checks, service assertions, remote verification, local signature verification, hardware-rooted verification, and unresolved verification are not interchangeable outcomes.

3. How hardware attestation and confidential-computing evidence map onto capture-boundary independence without overclaiming faithful software semantics.
4. Whether an external attester that participates in event selection becomes a candidate root for later consequences when its selection suppresses a required record.
5. How to represent threshold signing, quorum attestation, and multiple verifiers without collapsing channel-specific custody and dark surfaces.
6. Whether verification implementation status should be recorded per receipt, per deployment interval, or both.
7. How to preserve source-level inspection as provenance when a public branch changes after the architectural observation was made.

## Candidate cluster 3: Root identity, bonded-root qualification, and authority-to-accountability closure

### 26. Triggering observation and bounded source read

Larry Peseckis handed into MICRM development a public post by Emily Hartstone announcing completion of the Runtime Authority trilogy and describing its sequence as:

- *Before It Acts*: runtime authority for one machine taking one consequential action;
- *The Chain*: authority moving through machine-to-machine delegation and handoffs;
- *The Root*: a provable human source to which the chain traces back.

The public Runtime Authority Series page describes the same progression. It presents pre-execution authorization, authority continuity across machine delegation, root identity across organizational boundaries, and accountability when action occurs far from the originating enterprise.

A related public Runtime Authority Fabric paper describes a three-part architecture in which RAC governs the authority event, RAF governs authority across the agentic chain, and CORTHEM proves governed events and chains. It also states that individually authorized actions can create an unauthorized combined consequence and distinguishes authority to act from authority to authorize.

This is substantive architectural overlap with MICRM's authority axis. It is not sufficient evidence that the two models are identical.

The source read for this cluster is bounded to:

- the Emily Hartstone LinkedIn post supplied by Larry on 2026-07-17;
- Emily Hartstone, *The Runtime Authority Series* public books page, observed 2026-07-17;
- Hartstone Institute, *Runtime Authority Fabric: Governing Authority Across the Agentic Chain*, observed 2026-07-17;
- and the Hartstone Institute public venture descriptions for RAC, CORTHEM, and the human identity layer, observed 2026-07-17.

The public summaries establish the claims made on those pages. They do not establish the complete contents of the three books, and this register makes no absence claim about material not publicly inspected.

The public materials also describe two source levels. The Runtime Authority Fabric paper says that a human or system grants authority, while *The Root* says that every chain ends at a person. Those claims can coexist if an immediate authority source and an asserted ultimate identity root are different objects. MICRM should not collapse those levels without an explicit root rule.

### 27. Exact overlap and non-identity

The overlap is strongest at the level of authority structure:

```text
one consequential machine action
        -> authority event

machine-to-machine delegation
        -> authority chain

identified source of authority
        -> root
```

MICRM contains corresponding objects, but they are not interchangeable:

| Runtime Authority public formulation | MICRM object | Exact distinction |
|---|---|---|
| one action governed before consequence | one six-layer traversal | MICRM includes Intent, Context, Action Selection, Gate, Actuation, and a discrete Consequence, not only the authority decision |
| authority through a machine chain | run graph and cross-root seams | MICRM records actual traversal composition and separates authority continuity from accountability propagation |
| a person the chain traces back to | bonded root | MICRM requires downward authority and upward accountability to terminate at the same root; traceability to an identity is not yet a defined bonded-root qualification |
| proof that governance held | evidence spine and run-level records | MICRM types claim scope, channel emission, custody, residual dark surfaces, and exit evidence |
| combined effect of individually authorized actions | convergent or sequential multi-root consequence | MICRM additionally defines candidate roots, whole-consequence binding, per-path cuts, and structural exclusions |

The public Runtime Authority material is authority-centric and AI-enterprise focused. MICRM is consequence-centric and substrate-neutral. The overlap is real enough to constrain novelty claims, while the governed objects and normative mechanics remain distinct.

### 28. Candidate gap exposed in MICRM-v0.2

MICRM-v0.2 uses **bonded root** as a load-bearing term but does not provide a standalone qualification rule for a bonded root.

The current model establishes that:

- authority and accountability must terminate at the same bonded root;
- a run may contain multiple bonded roots;
- selected consequences may bind multiple roots;
- authority representations can cross seams;
- and root assignment can affect candidate membership and exit.

It does not yet define:

- whether a bonded root must be a human person, legal entity, institution, defined collective, or another accountability-bearing principal;
- what evidence distinguishes an identified person from a bonded root;
- whether presence, liveness, credential control, consent, authorization, delegation authority, and accountability-bearing status must be established separately;
- how a root remains continuous across organizational and technical handoffs;
- how composite or quorum authority maps to one root;
- or how proof of one originating person interacts with the second invariant when several roots share one consequence.

The Hartstone material therefore does not merely resemble MICRM. It pressure-tests the meaning of MICRM's own root.

### 29. Candidate terminology

#### 29.1 Root identity record

A **root identity record** is evidence identifying a person, legal entity, institution, defined collective, or other asserted principal at the resolution required for the incident.

A root identity record may include identity, presence, liveness, credential, device, organizational, or registry evidence. It does not by itself establish action authorization, delegation continuity, Actuation, authorship, or accountability bonding.

#### 29.2 Authority source

An **authority source** is the principal or authority-bearing structure from which the relevant permission, delegation, mandate, or jurisdiction descends for the selected traversal.

Identification of an authority source does not establish that the selected action remained within the granted scope or that the source bears the selected consequence.

#### 29.3 Accountability-bearing principal

An **accountability-bearing principal** is the person, entity, institution, or defined collective to which the accountability path is structurally assigned for the selected traversal or consequence under the applicable frame and overlays.

MICRM identifies the termination structure. The legal or regulatory basis for bearing accountability remains an overlay question.

#### 29.4 Bonded-root relation

A **bonded-root relation** is an independently supported structural finding that the downward authority path and upward accountability path for one traversal terminate at the same identified principal at the incident's selected resolution.

The root is not bonded merely because an identity is present at one end of the chain. Bonding is a relation among principal identity, authority termination, accountability termination, and same-principal continuity.

#### 29.5 Authority traceability

**Authority traceability** is the ability to reconstruct a path from a selected action or traversal to an asserted authority source.

Traceability is evidence about lineage. It is not equivalent to a bonded-root finding. A traceable chain may still be orphaned if the accountability path terminates elsewhere or nowhere.

#### 29.6 Root-continuity record

A **root-continuity record** preserves the principal identity, authority scope, delegation rights, time bounds, revocation state, jurisdiction, representations, and seam transformations needed to evaluate whether a root relation remained intact across the run graph.

Endpoint identity equality is insufficient where authority scope, principal capacity, or accountability assignment changed between the endpoints.

| accountability termination | independently supported assignment of the upward accountability path | identity of the authority source or same-principal closure |
| bonded-root termination | authority termination, accountability termination, and same-principal finding | legal fault, culpability, damages, or fractional allocation |

### 32. Candidate normative propositions

The following are candidate propositions for a later MICRM version. They are not ratified.

1. **Identity ceiling:** Proof of a person, entity, device holder, or credential controller establishes only the identity or presence claim within the evidenced scope. It does not by itself establish authorization, delegation continuity, Actuation, authorship, or accountability bonding.

2. **Traceability-not-bonding rule:** A chain that can be traced to an authority source is not thereby accountable. Bonded-root termination requires independently supported authority termination, accountability termination, and same-principal closure for the traversal.

3. **Bonded-root relation rule:** A bonded root is a structural relation, not an identifier. Root qualification depends on the evidenced connection between principal identity, downward authority, and upward accountability.

4. **Authority-to-authorize separation:** Authority to perform an action does not imply authority to delegate, create, expand, or transfer authority to another machine or principal. Delegation authority must be evidenced separately.

5. **Root-continuity rule:** Endpoint identity or lineage does not establish continuous root bonding across a run. Scope, time, revocation, jurisdiction, delegation rights, principal substitutions, and seam transformations must remain evidenced at the resolution required for the claim.

6. **Multi-root non-collapse:** Proof that one chain segment traces to one person or principal does not collapse the candidate set for a selected consequence. The second invariant continues to bind every candidate root unless every path from that root is cut or structurally excluded.

7. **Principal-type nonpresumption:** MICRM should not infer that a bonded root is human, organizational, institutional, collective, or machine merely from the identity mechanism or implementation vocabulary. Principal type and accountability-bearing capacity must be declared and evidenced. Whether a regime requires ultimate human termination belongs to an explicit core ruling or overlay, not an unstated assumption.

## Candidate cluster 4: Capability-authority separation, execution-path mediation, and delayed consequence recognition

### 37. Triggering observation and bounded source read

Larry Peseckis handed into MICRM development an exchange concerning a paralegal email that may waive legal privilege even where the mail system performs exactly as designed.

Larry's supplied comment separated technical success from institutional consequence:

- the email sends;
- transport and delivery evidence report success;
- no mail-stack component necessarily represents legal privilege;
- and a privilege consequence may be asserted or recognized later in a legal proceeding.

Emily Hartstone replied that the authority to act should be checked separately from the ability to act, and that the check belongs in the execution path rather than merely in the log. In a direct message supplied by Larry, she attributed the same formulation to him and described it as a strong statement of the Runtime Authority Control thesis.

The source read for this cluster is bounded to the exchange supplied by Larry on 2026-07-17. This register does not establish:

- the complete facts of the underlying paralegal example;
- whether privilege was legally waived in any particular matter;
- the full contents or implementation of RAC;
- or a general rule about when legal privilege attaches, is lost, or is restored.

The exchange is used as an architectural pressure test.

### 38. Exact architectural reading

The exchange exposes four distinctions that MICRM should not collapse.

#### 38.1 Capability is not authority

A system may possess the technical ability to perform an action without possessing authority for that selected action. It may also possess authority for an action it cannot successfully actuate.

The findings are logically separate even where one software component evaluates both.

```text
technical ability to act
        !=
authority to perform the selected action
```

The phrase "checked separately" should therefore be read as a requirement for separate claims and evidence, not necessarily separate hardware, services, or code paths.

#### 38.2 Evidence is not mediation

A log may show that an authorization service ran, that a policy outcome was produced, or that an action executed. None of those records establishes that the action was structurally unable to proceed without the authority outcome.

"In the execution path" is a causal property:

> Actuation was conditional on a qualifying control outcome produced before Actuation.

It is not established by component naming, network position, timestamp proximity, or the existence of an audit record.

A record channel may participate in a Gate where a recorded or attested outcome is consumed as a required input to the final control decision. A component called a log is not categorically outside the Gate, and a component called an authorization service is not categorically inside it.

The exact distinction is:

```text
record of an authority decision
        !=
authority mediation of the selected Actuation
```

#### 38.3 Technical consequence is not overlay classification

A mail system can strongly evidence transport, delivery, recipient, timestamp, and content bytes while remaining unable to determine whether the disclosure altered legal privilege.

The technical record may establish an underlying state change. The legal or institutional consequence class may depend on facts and rules outside the mail substrate, including recipient role, matter context, jurisdiction, precautions, remediation, later argument, and adjudication.

This does not mean software can never govern privilege-sensitive transmission. It means a Gate cannot govern a consequence class that is absent from its protective scope or unavailable through consequence-relevant Context and authority representations.

#### 38.4 Delayed effect and delayed recognition are different

The statement that a consequence "arrives months later" can describe several different structures:

1. the consequence occurred at disclosure but was discovered later;
2. the underlying state change occurred at disclosure and a legal classification was asserted later;
3. the legal status changed only through a later institutional act;
4. the effect accumulated over time;
5. or the consequence timing remains contested.

MICRM should not infer one of those structures from elapsed time alone.

### 39. Candidate terminology

#### 39.1 Execution capability

**Execution capability** is the structural ability of a machine, principal, or system configuration to perform a class of Actuation under stated conditions.

Capability evidence may include interfaces, credentials, reachable tools, resource state, physical capacity, and successful execution. Successful Actuation establishes capability for the selected event under the observed conditions. It does not by itself establish continuing capability, general capability, or authority.

#### 39.2 Selected-action authority

**Selected-action authority** is an independently supported, scoped authority finding for the action instance represented at the incident's selected resolution, including consequence-relevant parameters, targets, recipients, content, timing, jurisdiction, and authority representations.

A generic role permission or technical permission may support this finding. It does not complete it where the selected action differs in a consequence-relevant parameter.

#### 39.3 Authority mediation

**Authority mediation** is the structural relation in which Actuation of the selected action is conditional on a qualifying authority-control outcome produced before Actuation.

Authority mediation requires evidence of:

- the selected action presented for evaluation;
- the authority rule, scope, and representations evaluated;
- the control outcome;
- the causal dependency between that outcome and Actuation;
- any available bypass or fail-open path;
- and the binding from the outcome to the actual Actuation identity.

A policy evaluation that does not constrain Actuation is advisory or observational for that event, not authority mediation.

#### 39.4 Authorization record

An **authorization record** is evidence that an authority claim, policy evaluation, control outcome, or related event was recorded.

The record may evidence authority mediation. It does not establish mediation merely by existing.

#### 39.5 Underlying state change

An **underlying state change** is the directly evidenced change produced by Actuation at the resolution of the observed substrate.

Examples include:

- message delivered;
- file disclosed;
- payment transferred;
- physical object moved;
- legal document filed;
- or institutional status field changed.

An underlying state change may support one or more consequence assertions. It does not select the governing consequence class by itself.

#### 39.6 Consequence assertion

A **consequence assertion** is a claim that one or more underlying state changes instantiate a named consequence class under a stated model, overlay, policy, or institutional rule.

A consequence assertion records its source, rule basis, incident resolution, and evidentiary status. Assertion does not equal recognition or adjudication.

#### 39.7 Consequence recognition event

A **consequence recognition event** is an identified event by which an authorized or institutionally relevant principal accepts, rejects, modifies, or leaves unresolved a consequence assertion.

The event may be machine-initiated, human, institutional, judicial, regulatory, contractual, or mixed. It is represented as a MICRM traversal only when it is itself a machine-initiated action. Otherwise it remains an externally evidenced event linked to the consequence record.

#### 39.8 Consequence timing tuple

A **consequence timing tuple** separates:

```text
Actuation time
underlying-state-change time
discovery time
assertion time
recognition or adjudication time
overlay-effective time
```

The times may coincide or differ. A later assertion or adjudication does not by itself establish that the consequence became effective only at the later time.

### 40. Claim-separation table

| Claim | Evidence that may support it | What the claim does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| execution capability | reachable interface, credential, tool, resource, physical capacity, successful Actuation | authority, rightful use, or consequence class |
| generic permission | role, policy, entitlement, delegation, organizational mandate | authority for every parameterized action instance |
| selected-action authority | scoped grant and authority representations covering the selected action at incident resolution | actual Gate mediation, Actuation, or benign consequence |
| authority evaluation occurred | per-event evaluation and outcome evidence | that Actuation depended on the result |
| authority mediation | structural dependency from qualifying control outcome to Actuation | correctness of the rule, complete protective scope, or consequence absence |
| authorization record | authenticated or witnessed record of authority-related activity | mediation, completeness, or faithful observation outside attestation scope |
| Actuation | tool, transport, actuator, transaction, filing, or execution evidence | authority or final consequence classification |
| technical success | protocol, transaction, or infrastructure success within its event class | legal, contractual, ethical, safety, or institutional success |
| underlying state change | direct substrate observation and custody evidence | which overlay-defined consequence class applies |
| consequence assertion | identified rule, source, evidence, and asserted class | final recognition, effective time, or uncontested truth |
| consequence recognition | authorized or institutionally relevant decision accepting or rejecting the assertion | that the underlying state change occurred at the recognition time |
| delayed recognition | later discovery, assertion, or decision | delayed occurrence of the underlying effect |

### 41. Candidate normative propositions

The following propositions are candidate architecture for a later MICRM version. They are not ratified.

1. **Capability-authority orthogonality:** Execution capability and selected-action authority are separate findings. Evidence of either does not establish the other.

2. **Parameterized-authority rule:** Authority is evaluated for the selected action at the incident's chosen resolution. Generic capability, role membership, or permission for an action class does not establish authority for consequence-relevant parameters that were not within scope.

3. **In-path mediation rule:** A pre-Actuation authority check governs a selected Actuation only when independently supported structural evidence establishes that Actuation was conditional on the check's qualifying outcome. Physical placement, service naming, and record timestamps are insufficient.

4. **Record-control non-equivalence:** A post-Actuation log, non-blocking advisory decision, or unconsumed authority record cannot retroactively authorize the completed Actuation. A record channel may participate in mediation only where its outcome is causally consumed before Actuation.

5. **Semantic-reach rule:** A Gate cannot claim consequence-relevant authority mediation for a consequence class unless the required authority and semantic representations reached the Gate and the Gate had structural control capacity for that class. Technical success does not establish that an unrepresented consequence class was absent.

6. **Technical-success ceiling:** Successful transport, protocol completion, transaction confirmation, or infrastructure health establishes only the technical claims within the evidenced event class. It does not establish authority, institutional validity, or absence of a later or externally classified consequence.

7. **State-change and consequence-class separation:** MICRM records the underlying state change separately from any overlay or institutional classification of that change. The selected consequence must identify which object is being evaluated.

8. **Delayed-recognition non-shift:** Later discovery, assertion, or recognition does not by itself move the occurrence or effective time of the selected consequence. Timing is an independently supported finding under the applicable consequence model or overlay.

9. **Cross-substrate continuity:** A technically successful Actuation may become Context for a later institutional, legal, financial, or safety process. A substrate transition or delay does not erase the ancestor path or release an otherwise bound root.

### 43. Privilege-waiver worked specimen

The email example should not be represented as one undifferentiated consequence.

#### 43.1 Technical traversal

```text
Intent:
  transmit a message for a legal matter

Context:
  message content, recipient, matter state,
  privilege representations, role, policy, and prior communications

Action Selection:
  send this message and attachment to this recipient

Gate:
  final authority, content, recipient, and policy controls actually applied

Actuation:
  message submitted and delivered

Immediate underlying state changes:
  recipient obtained the message;
  information crossed the sender's prior control boundary
```

Clean SMTP, transport, authentication, and delivery evidence may establish the Actuation and immediate state changes. It does not establish that the selected disclosure was authorized under the relevant legal or institutional rule.

#### 43.2 Consequence-class alternatives

The selected consequence may be any of the following, depending on the investigation and overlay:

- unauthorized disclosure;
- loss of confidentiality control;
- exposure to a privilege-waiver claim;
- privilege legally treated as waived;
- litigation disadvantage resulting from the disclosure;
- or a later institutional decision accepting or rejecting waiver.

Those are not interchangeable consequence nodes.

#### 43.3 Later legal process

A later lawyer, court, regulator, or institution may assert or recognize a legal consequence. Where that later event is machine-initiated, it may be represented as another traversal whose Context includes the email disclosure. Where it is human or institutional without a machine-initiated action, it remains an external recognition event linked to the selected consequence record.

The later room does not erase the original path. It changes the evidence and consequence-resolution problem.

### 44. Pressure tests

#### 44.1 Generic send permission, unauthorized disclosure

A paralegal is permitted to send email and has technical access to the mail system. The selected recipient and privileged attachment fall outside authorized scope.

Finding:

- execution capability may be established;
- generic email permission may be established;
- selected-action authority is excluded or unresolved;
- successful delivery does not cure the authority defect.

#### 44.2 Authority established, capability absent

A principal is authorized to transmit a filing, but the destination system is unavailable and no Actuation occurs.

Finding:

- selected-action authority may be established;
- execution capability for the selected event is excluded or unresolved;
- no completed Actuation follows merely from valid authority.

#### 44.3 Post-hoc approval in a log

An action executes. A reviewer approves it afterward, and the approval is logged.

Finding:

- the log may evidence later ratification or institutional response;
- it did not mediate the completed Actuation;
- whether the later approval has legal or organizational effect belongs to the applicable overlay;
- it cannot be treated as a pre-Actuation Gate outcome for that traversal.

#### 44.4 Synchronous record consumed by a Gate

An authorization service emits a signed permit record. The actuator verifies and requires that permit before execution.

Finding:

- the record channel participates in authority mediation;
- the signed record remains an evidence object;
- mediation still requires binding from the evaluated selected action to the permit and from the permit to the actual Actuation.

#### 44.5 Policy service present but fail-open

A policy service evaluates the action and emits a denial, but a fail-open path allows Actuation.

Finding:

- evaluation and denial are established;
- authority mediation is excluded for the actual path;
- the record does not convert the bypassed control into an effective Gate.

#### 44.6 Privilege semantics absent from Context

A mail Gate checks malware, recipient domain, and transport policy but receives no privilege classification or matter context.

Finding:

- the Gate may be structurally effective for its declared classes;
- it cannot establish authority mediation for privilege-sensitive disclosure;
- the legal consequence may still arise even though every declared technical control succeeds.

#### 44.7 Delayed discovery

A disclosure creates an immediate loss of control, but no party discovers it for six months.

Finding:

- discovery time is later than the underlying-state-change time;
- the delay does not establish that the disclosure consequence began six months later.

#### 44.8 Delayed institutional effect

A filing or ruling months later changes legal status based on the prior disclosure.

Finding:

- the later legal status change may be a distinct consequence event;
- the prior disclosure is Context or an ancestor event;
- the effective time and causal relationship require the governing overlay and evidence.

#### 44.9 Waiver assertion rejected

An opposing party argues waiver, and the authorized decision-maker rejects the argument.

Finding:

- the waiver assertion and recognition event are evidenced;
- privilege waiver is not established merely because the assertion occurred;
- other consequences, including disclosure or litigation cost, may remain.

### 45. Interaction with existing MICRM architecture

This cluster does not replace the canonical Gate definition. It sharpens what must be shown when a Gate is described as an authority check.

MICRM-v0.2 already requires:

- a positional Gate after Action Selection and before Actuation;
- actual per-event Gate evaluation and outcome evidence;
- outcome linkage to Actuation;
- declared protective scope;
- representation reach;
- and separation of Actuation from Consequence.

The cluster adds candidate language for capability-authority orthogonality and causal mediation. It also begins resolving the v0.2 Deferred Register item concerning consequence-node and incident-resolution selection.

The cluster interacts with prior v0.3 candidates as follows:

- **Cluster 2, attestation scope:** a record may prove its own integrity without proving that authority mediated Actuation or that a legal consequence was absent.
- **Cluster 3, bonded-root qualification:** selected-action authority must terminate through the root relation, but root traceability does not prove capability or mediation.
- **Cluster 1, external probes:** a diagnostic test can evaluate whether a Gate recognizes privilege-sensitive cases, but probe behavior does not automatically generalize to ordinary operation.

### 46. Related-work and strategic disposition

The exchange is important because it states a boundary in language that maps cleanly onto MICRM:

```text
ability
  -> capability and possible Actuation

authority
  -> downward authority path and Gate evaluation

execution-path check
  -> causal authority mediation before Actuation

log
  -> evidence of events within its capture scope
```

MICRM should not claim to be the first architecture to separate capability from authorization or to require pre-execution authority controls. Hartstone's public work is relevant adjacent architecture on that axis.

MICRM's distinct contribution remains the consequence-centered frame:

- it binds authority to accountability through a bonded root;
- separates Gate, Actuation, and Consequence;
- types the evidence needed to show that a control actually mediated execution;
- follows paths across roots and substrates;
- and records unresolved consequence classification without converting uncertainty into a factual finding.

The privilege specimen also shows why substrate neutrality is load-bearing. The selected consequence may not be visible to the substrate that performed the Actuation.

### 47. Open construction questions

1. Should capability-authority orthogonality become a core claim-separation rule or remain explanatory vocabulary?
2. What minimum causal evidence establishes that a Gate outcome was required for Actuation across software, financial, physical, and institutional substrates?
3. How should fail-open, emergency, break-glass, degraded-mode, and cached authorization paths be represented?
4. At what resolution must a selected action be parameterized before authority can be evaluated without making every byte or physical variable normative?
5. Does the consequence-resolution record belong in the core run graph, an overlay, or a shared boundary record?
6. How should MICRM represent a consequence whose legal or institutional effective time is retroactive, contested, or jurisdiction-dependent?
7. When does a later recognition event become a new selected consequence rather than evidence about an earlier one?
8. What evidence distinguishes delayed effect from delayed discovery or delayed recognition?
9. How should a Gate declare protective scope for consequence classes whose semantics are supplied by an overlay or external classification service?
10. When does a classification provider that supplies privilege, safety, or regulatory context attach its own root to a later consequence through omission, error, or authority representation?

### 51. Candidate terminology

#### 51.1 Output attempt

An **output attempt** is an identified generation or rendering attempt associated with one user request or machine-selected continuation.

Distinct attempts may share the same visible prompt while differing in:

- model;
- configuration;
- Context;
- hidden policy state;
- sampling state;
- routing;
- tools;
- stop reason;
- or initiating principal.

An output attempt is not presumed to be a separate MICRM traversal until the incident resolution and available evidence establish a separate machine-initiated action or state-changing event.

#### 51.2 Provisional output segment

A **provisional output segment** is content exposed outside the generating component before the platform has designated the complete output as final or committed.

The label provisional describes lifecycle status. It does not mean consequence-free.

#### 51.3 User-visible Actuation

**User-visible Actuation** occurs when machine-generated content changes the state of an interface available to a user or another consuming system.

Once a segment is rendered outside the generating component, the segment may already have produced consequences, including:

- disclosure;
- cognitive influence;
- copied or acted-upon instructions;
- accessibility-tool capture;
- notification content;
- downstream automation;
- or independent recording.

Later erasure does not establish that the earlier Actuation or consequence did not occur.

#### 51.4 Stream replacement event

A **stream replacement event** is a machine-initiated state change that removes, supersedes, or ceases presenting one visible output attempt and presents another output attempt or version in its place.

The term does not assign the cause to a classifier, model, client, network, or provider unless separately evidenced.

#### 51.5 Committed output

A **committed output** is the output version the platform designates for durable retention, transcript inclusion, downstream Context admission, or final delivery.

Committed status does not imply that prior provisional segments were never visible or consequential.

#### 51.6 Output lineage

**Output lineage** is the evidenced relation among:

- the initiating request;
- output attempts;
- streamed segments;
- stop or intervention events;
- discarded or superseded versions;
- the committed output;
- and later transcript or Context records.

A final transcript without output lineage may be an accurate record of the committed output while remaining incomplete as a record of the user-visible run.

#### 51.7 Retry initiation

**Retry initiation** is the machine- or human-initiated action that causes a prior output attempt to be repeated, replaced, resumed, or regenerated.

Candidate initiating principals include:

- the user;
- the application client;
- an orchestration service;
- a safety or policy control;
- a routing or availability service;
- the model or agent under delegated authority;
- or another root.

Numerical attempt separation does not establish accountable initiation. The authority path and bonded root of the retry must be evidenced.

#### 51.8 Witness opportunity

A **witness opportunity** is the interval and interface condition under which an event can be observed or captured by a specified witness channel.

Witness opportunity is not equivalent to actual observation. A user may be present but not perceive the event, or absent while an independent recorder captures it.

#### 51.9 Semantic delta

A **semantic delta** is the evidenced difference between two output versions at the level of consequence-relevant claims, instructions, qualifications, confidence, omissions, or authority representations.

Word overlap, thematic similarity, and embedding similarity may support comparison. None is a complete semantic finding by itself.

### 52. Traversal resolution for incremental output

A streaming interface exposes a resolution problem that a final-answer-only record can hide.

#### 52.1 Whole-response representation

At coarse resolution, one response may be represented as one traversal whose Actuation is the committed output.

That representation is accountability-equivalent only where provisional segments did not create a separately relevant state change, consequence, authority transition, or evidence-custody difference.

#### 52.2 Incremental representation

Where content becomes externally observable before completion, MICRM should use one of two evidence-compatible representations:

1. ordered Actuation events inside one traversal, with every visible segment and revision preserved; or
2. separate consequence-bearing traversals for output attempts, visible segments, replacement, and commitment where those events independently change state or accountability.

The selected representation must preserve:

- ordering;
- content identity;
- visibility;
- replacement;
- authority and control events;
- and any consequence already produced.

#### 52.3 Gate timing under streaming

A control that acts before segment `S` becomes externally visible may Gate that segment.

A control that acts after segment `S` became visible cannot retroactively become the Gate for `S`. It may:

- Gate later segments;
- stop further Actuation;
- initiate a retraction or replacement traversal;
- or reduce later consequence.

It cannot erase the fact that `S` was actuated.

This preserves the canonical positional relation:

```text
Action Selection
        ↓
Gate for the selected segment or action
        ↓
Actuation
        ↓
Consequence
```

A streaming system may repeat that sequence at segment resolution.

### 54. Candidate normative propositions

The following propositions are candidate architecture for a later MICRM version. They are not ratified.

1. **Provisional-Actuation rule:** Content becomes an Actuation when it changes an external interface or consuming state, even if the platform labels the content provisional and later removes it.

2. **Retraction non-erasure:** Retraction, replacement, or transcript omission does not negate a prior Actuation or consequence. The retraction or replacement is a later state-changing event with its own authority, evidence, and accountability path.

3. **Incremental-Gate rule:** In a streaming system, a control may Gate only content or actions that have not yet been actuated. A post-display intervention may stop future output or initiate remediation, but it cannot retroactively Gate already displayed content.

4. **Output-lineage rule:** Where several attempts or versions contribute to the user-visible run, the accountability record must preserve their ordering, identity, replacement relations, initiating principals, control events, and retention status. A committed-output-only transcript is insufficient for claims about the complete run.

5. **Silent-replacement ceiling:** A witnessed wipe-and-restart establishes a user-visible replacement event. It does not by itself establish regeneration, classifier intervention, model identity, retry initiation, or the responsible root.

6. **Retry-authority rule:** A user retry, an application retry, a policy-triggered retry, and a model-selected retry are distinct actions. Each requires its own authority path, initiating principal, and evidence. Operational similarity does not collapse their accountability posture.

7. **Semantic-delta rule:** The accountability effect of output replacement is determined at the level of consequence-relevant claims and capacities, not theme or wording alone. A replacement may preserve a broad conclusion while changing a caveat, refusal, authority representation, instruction, confidence, or actionable detail.

8. **Witness-availability rule:** A transient user-interface channel cannot support durable claims about replaced output unless the workflow provides an evidenced witness and retention path. Product design that permits users to leave during generation reduces contemporaneous human witness opportunity, but does not by itself establish concealment, intent, or absence of provider-side records.

9. **Final-transcript ceiling:** The retained transcript establishes the committed conversation state within its capture scope. It does not establish that no provisional output, discarded attempt, control event, or replacement occurred before commitment.

10. **Cause-before-attribution rule:** A control or component is attributed as the cause of a replacement only where independently supported evidence links its event and outcome to the replacement transition. Temporal proximity and topic correlation are insufficient.

Finding:

Two wipe-and-restart events occur during discussion of safety intervention.

Finding:

Finding:

### 57. Candidate observation protocol

A purpose-built validation capture should separate the interface event from its hidden cause.

Minimum protocol:

1. Use benign prompts that do not seek harmful or disallowed content.
2. Predeclare the hypotheses, event classes, and scoring categories.
3. Record product surface, model label if displayed, browser or application version, settings, date, time, and network conditions.
4. Start screen capture before submitting the prompt and retain the complete attempt.
5. Record whether the user initiated any retry, edit, stop, or regeneration action.
6. Preserve the partial and committed outputs through frame capture or transcription with timestamps.
7. Score content change through a predeclared semantic-delta scheme, not theme alone.
8. Record every prompt in the denominator, including runs with no replacement event.
9. Randomize or alternate topic classes if testing topic association.
10. Repeat across sessions only where contamination and model or product changes are disclosed.
11. Treat browser developer logs or network archives as sensitive because they may contain credentials, conversation content, or identifiers. Do not publish raw captures without sanitization and custody review.
12. State the supported finding narrowly: the protocol can establish rendered behavior and frequencies under the tested conditions. It cannot establish the hidden trigger without provider-side or other independent evidence.

This protocol extends the externally anchored probe architecture in cluster 1. The recording and scoring process are themselves accountable traversals.

## Candidate cluster 6: Confidential auditability, execution binding, and manipulation-chain inclusion

### 61. Triggering observation and bounded source

Larry Peseckis identified a general audit problem that remains after output lineage, attestation scope, and record-channel mechanics are separated:

- proprietary models, classifiers, prompts, policy systems, compiled artifacts, and transformation pipelines may be protected from direct disclosure;
- meaningful audit must still be possible without treating trade-secret or restricted status as an exemption from evidence;
- evidence that a model, policy, or binary was inspected does not establish that the inspected artifact produced the selected consequence;
- and every component that can alter consequence-relevant data or its retained record belongs inside the auditable run, including classifiers.

The source for this cluster is the dialogue supplied by Larry on 2026-07-17. It does not make a claim about the internal architecture, retention practice, classifier behavior, or audit interface of any particular provider.

### 62. Existing coverage and remaining gap

Canonical MICRM-v0.2 and the preceding v0.3 candidate clusters already establish that:

- evidence strength is claim-bounded and every channel retains a residual dark surface;
- attestation of a record does not establish independent observation of the underlying execution;
- runtime activation must be evidenced separately from source presence or configuration availability;
- Gate outcome, Actuation, and Consequence are separate findings;
- output attempts, classifier events, retries, replacements, transformations, and retention changes may require separate lineage;
- and confidentiality does not convert unresolved evidence into a factual finding.

The remaining gap is a general rule for auditing a system whose implementation cannot be disclosed directly. MICRM needs to distinguish:

```text
implementation visibility
        from
auditability of a bounded claim

artifact reviewed
        from
artifact executed

component classified as internal
        from
component outside the accountable run
```

### 63. Candidate terminology

#### 63.1 Audit claim

An **audit claim** is a precisely stated proposition about an identified system, artifact, event, path, control, or consequence that an audit process is intended to establish, exclude, or leave unresolved.

An audit claim must identify:

- the selected event or incident;
- the claim type;
- the required resolution;
- the evidence and verification method;
- the party making the claim;
- and the residual dark surface.

A general statement that a system is audited, certified, safe, compliant, or verified is not a complete audit claim.

#### 63.2 Protected implementation detail

A **protected implementation detail** is information whose disclosure is restricted by trade-secret, contractual, privacy, legal-privilege, national-security, safety, or comparable constraints.

Protected status may constrain:

- who may inspect the detail;
- where inspection may occur;
- what may be published;
- and which verification mechanism is available.

Protected status does not establish the truth of a claim and does not lower the evidence required for an accountability exit.

#### 63.3 Claim-bounded audit interface

A **claim-bounded audit interface** is a technical, institutional, or hybrid mechanism that permits an authorized verifier to evaluate a specified audit claim without requiring unrestricted disclosure of every protected implementation detail.

Possible implementations include:

- restricted clean-room inspection;
- regulator or cleared-auditor access;
- sealed evidence packages;
- selective disclosure;
- hardware-backed attestation;
- cryptographic commitments;
- zero-knowledge or proof-carrying mechanisms;
- reproducible challenge-response testing;
- or another independently supportable method.

The interface is not presumed sufficient merely because one of these labels applies. Its scope, dependencies, custody, and residual dark surface must be evidenced.

#### 63.4 Execution binding

**Execution binding** is the evidenced relation between a reviewed, approved, committed, or attested artifact and the actual runtime event that produced or altered the selected Actuation or Consequence.

Execution binding may require evidence of:

- artifact identity;
- configuration identity;
- load or deployment event;
- runtime activation;
- request or traversal linkage;
- control and transformation lineage;
- and the relationship to Actuation.

A model card, source review, policy approval, binary hash, certificate, or audit report does not by itself establish execution binding.

#### 63.5 Manipulation chain

A **manipulation chain** is the ordered set of components and transitions that observe, label, filter, transform, suppress, route, replace, enrich, redact, authorize, or determine retention of consequence-relevant data or state.

Candidate component classes include:

- input classifiers;
- prompt and Context constructors;
- retrieval and memory systems;
- model routers;
- primary and fallback models;
- during-generation classifiers;
- output filters and redactors;
- tool-policy systems;
- retry and recovery services;
- transcript and retention systems;
- interface renderers;
- and audit or attestation adapters.

A component belongs to the manipulation chain because of its evidenced effect, not because of its product name or organizational label.

#### 63.6 Audit boundary

The **audit boundary** is the set of roots, components, transformations, evidence channels, and interfaces whose behavior must be represented to evaluate the selected audit claim.

A component that can change the claim-bearing state, the authority applied to it, the Actuation, or the retained evidence cannot be excluded from the audit boundary merely because it is described as internal, auxiliary, safety-related, compiled, or proprietary.

#### 63.7 Artifact-transformation lineage

**Artifact-transformation lineage** is the evidenced sequence linking an originating artifact to every consequence-relevant derived artifact and to the runtime artifact actually used.

Examples include:

```text
source model
    -> conversion
    -> optimization
    -> quantization
    -> packaging
    -> signing
    -> distribution
    -> loading
    -> runtime execution
```

The lineage records identity, transformation, authority, custody, and residual uncertainty at each transition. It does not presume semantic equivalence merely because the transformation was authorized or reproducible.

#### 63.8 Verifier-dependence profile

A **verifier-dependence profile** identifies the parties, systems, keys, hardware, software, records, and assumptions on which a verification result depends.

A verifier may be independent in custody or signing while remaining dependent on the evaluated root for event observation, record construction, event selection, or field completeness.

### 64. Claim-separation table

| Claim | Evidence that may support it | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| a particular artifact was reviewed | review record, artifact commitment, reviewer identity and scope | that the artifact executed |
| the reviewed artifact executed | load and runtime evidence bound to the selected event | source approval, model card, or matching product label alone |
| a classifier evaluated an event | per-event classifier record linked to the evaluated state | the classifier being configured or available |
| the classifier outcome changed the run | causal link from outcome to suppression, routing, retry, authorization, or retention action | classifier outcome alone |
| a compiled artifact derives from a reviewed source | witnessed transformation lineage and identity commitments | matching version name or vendor assertion |
| a confidential auditor verified a claim | scoped auditor finding, procedure, evidence access, dependencies, and limitations | the word audited without the claim and scope |
| an attestation is valid | identified verification procedure and successful result | a field named valid or certificate presence alone |
| a proprietary system is auditable | a claim-bounded audit interface with execution binding and disclosed dark surface | refusal to disclose implementation details by itself |
| no relevant manipulation occurred | complete manipulation-chain evidence at the required resolution | inspection of only the primary model output |
| a root may exit accountability | every applicable exit condition established under the ordinary MICRM burden | confidentiality, certification, or proprietary status alone |

### 65. Candidate normative propositions

The following propositions are candidate architecture for a later MICRM version. They are not ratified.

1. **Confidentiality non-exemption:** Proprietary, privileged, classified, or otherwise restricted status may constrain disclosure but does not reduce the evidentiary burden for authority, accountability, a qualifying cut, or structural exclusion.

2. **Claim-bounded verification:** A restricted system may satisfy an audit obligation without unrestricted disclosure only where an independently supported mechanism verifies the specific claim at issue, identifies its scope and dependencies, and discloses the residual dark surface.

3. **Execution-binding rule:** Evidence that an artifact was reviewed, approved, signed, committed, or attested does not establish that it produced the selected event. The evidence must bind the artifact and configuration to the actual runtime traversal or Actuation.

4. **Manipulation-chain inclusion:** Every component that observes, labels, filters, transforms, suppresses, routes, replaces, authorizes, or determines retention of consequence-relevant state is part of the auditable run at the resolution required for the selected claim.

5. **Classifier participation rule:** A classifier is placed according to its evidenced function in the selected run. It may be a Context-layer Control, Gate-stage Control, Action Selection participant, Actuation-layer Control, evidence transformer, or several of these. The label classifier does not make it external to accountability.

6. **Transformation-lineage rule:** Compilation, conversion, optimization, quantization, packaging, routing, or loading does not break accountability continuity. A claim about the runtime artifact requires an evidenced lineage from the reviewed or authorized artifact to the artifact that actually executed.

7. **Alternative-verification ceiling:** A clean room, auditor statement, attestation, cryptographic proof, enclave, or selective-disclosure mechanism establishes only the claims inside its verified scope. It does not silently extend to unobserved events, omitted fields, causal explanations, or Consequences.

8. **Record-action separation:** A classifier label, authorization decision, or audit record is distinct from the downstream action taken because of it. Each consequence-relevant transformation, suppression, retry, routing, or retention action requires its own evidence and authority path.

9. **Audit-boundary completeness:** An audit of the primary model is insufficient where another component can alter the request, Context, selected action, Actuation, or retained record. The boundary must include every component capable of carrying the alleged contribution class.

10. **Confidentiality cannot exculpate:** Where the evidence required for an exit cannot be made available through direct or alternative verification, the exit remains unestablished. The result is continued binding by burden, not a factual finding of wrongdoing.

### 72. Author scope ruling: claim-bounded functional mediation boundary

> **Claim-bounded functional mediation boundary:** For a selected consequence, audit claim, or accountability exit, the run representation must include every identified component whose evidenced function altered consequence-relevant state, authority, Actuation, or retained evidence. ### 74. Claim-selection authority seam

The audit boundary in cluster 6 is claim-relative. That prevents a universal and unbounded requirement to audit every component at every resolution. It also makes selection of the claim and its required resolution load-bearing.

Selecting an audit claim can determine:

- which consequence is selected;
- which components enter the manipulation chain;
- which evidence channels are required;
- which roots become candidates;
- which dark surfaces must be bounded;
- which claims remain outside the audit;
- and whether an accountability exit can be evaluated.

Claim selection is therefore not neutral scoping. It is an authority-bearing decision over the audit frame.

Larry Peseckis ratified **investigative-frame authority** as a core candidate class:

> **Investigative-frame authority:** authority to determine the question evaluated, selected consequence, reference condition, event class, required resolution, included and excluded components, and the evidence from which the resulting finding may be drawn.

Probe selection and incident-claim selection are separate operations under this shared authority class. They require distinct operational records because they occur at different moments and create different immediate consequences, but both must identify the selecting principal, bonded root, authority source, exclusions, conflicts, challenge path, and dark surface created by the frame.

An overlay may determine which office, regulator, investigator, court, safety authority, or other principal may exercise this authority. MICRM must still record that the authority was exercised and what it changed. An interested root cannot establish its own exit by unilaterally narrowing the frame.

The author ruling establishes the candidate class. Exact draft placement, record integration, and cross-substrate pressure testing remain prerequisites to draft admission.

- who may select or narrow the claim;
- which bonded root authorizes that selection;
- whether affected roots may challenge the selection;
- what prevents an interested party from choosing a claim that excludes its own manipulation component;
- or when claim selection must be represented as its own traversal.

### 81. Candidate proposition 7.1: proposal-bound governance decision

> **r13 closure (see section 86A):** Frozen proposition 7.1 is SUPERSEDED. A governance decision applies only to the exact proposed transition or action identity to which it was bound.

The binding record should identify, at the resolution required for the selected consequence:

- proposal or action identifier;
- proposing principal and bonded root;
- target and receiving interface;
- tenant, jurisdiction, or operating domain where relevant;
- consequence-relevant parameters and payload identity;
- authority source and scope;
- evidence set or state references evaluated;
- policy and configuration identity;
- validity conditions and expiration;
- and the final Gate outcome.

A permit, refusal, or modification decision detached from one or more required binding dimensions does not govern a later Actuation merely because the two are semantically similar or belong to the same action class.

A transformed proposal requires a new binding determination unless independently supported evidence establishes that the transformation preserved every consequence-relevant binding dimension.

### 82. Candidate proposition 7.2: Gate-to-Actuation validity continuity

Larry Peseckis confirmed this proposition as an integration-ready core spine candidate.

> **Gate-to-Actuation validity continuity:** A Gate outcome governs an Actuation only where independently supported evidence establishes that every consequence-relevant authority, policy, target, evidence, and system-state condition on which the outcome depended remained valid until the Actuation became operationally real, or was protected by an accountability-equivalent binding mechanism that made an intervening change unable to invalidate the outcome.
>
> Where a required condition changed, expired, was revoked, or remains unresolved, the prior Gate outcome cannot establish authority for that Actuation. A new Gate evaluation or an accountability-equivalent control is required.

The Gate remains the final evaluative stage before Actuation. This proposition governs the validity of the Gate result over the interval between final Gate outcome and Actuation. It does not move the Gate and does not require continuous reevaluation.

Validity continuity may be established through atomic execution, transaction locking, a capability bound to the exact proposal and target, version-locked state, a permit whose validity interval includes Actuation, a revocation check, compare-and-swap or an accountability-equivalent state binding, or revalidation before execution.

Required findings:

| Finding | Operational result |
|---|---|
| Validity continuity established | The Gate outcome may support Actuation and a qualifying cut, subject to every other condition. |
| Validity continuity failed | The prior Gate outcome does not govern the Actuation. |
| Validity continuity unresolved | Gate governance and the claimed exit remain unestablished. Uncertainty alone does not establish unauthorized execution. |

The root asserting Gate governance, severance, or another accountability exit bears the burden.

### 83. Candidate proposition 7.3: bypass-complete control claim

A control is non-bypassable for a selected consequence only when independently supported evidence establishes that every evidence-compatible path capable of producing the relevant Actuation must pass through the control or an accountability-equivalent control.

The bypass analysis must include, where relevant:

- alternate interfaces;
- privileged or emergency routes;
- direct tool or actuator access;
- retries and fallback paths;
- stale sessions or cached grants;
- administrative overrides;
- shadow or legacy workflows;
- and cross-root handoffs that can reach the same Actuation.

A documented primary path does not establish non-bypassability. An unresolved evidence-compatible bypass path blocks the control claim by burden without proving that the bypass was used.

### 86. Pressure tests

#### 86.1 Correct refusal, ignored by actuator

A policy service records `deny`, but the actuator proceeds through a direct interface.

Finding: the refusal record is valid evidence that a decision occurred. It does not establish control of Actuation. Non-bypassability and Gate-to-Actuation correspondence fail.

#### 86.2 Valid permit, stale state

An action is authorized while the target is valid. Before Actuation, the target changes or the authority is revoked.

Finding: the earlier permit does not govern the later Actuation unless the freshness rule establishes continued validity at the boundary.

#### 86.3 Same action class, different target

A permit covers one transfer target. A semantically similar transfer is sent to another target.

Finding: action-class similarity does not preserve proposal binding. A new Gate determination is required.

#### 86.4 Clean primary path, privileged bypass

The documented workflow crosses a qualifying Gate, but an administrative route reaches the same actuator without it.

Finding: the control is not established as non-bypassable for the selected consequence. If use of the bypass is unresolved, the bypass claim remains unresolved rather than proven.

#### 86.5 Valid live-state Gate, contaminated continuity

The final Gate correctly evaluates current state, but the proposal's authority or semantic content was altered during an upstream handoff.

Finding: binding execution control does not repair failed upstream continuity. The conjunction fails even though the final Gate operated as designed.

#### 86.6 Clean continuity, advisory Gate

The proposal arrives with intact authority and evidence, but the final control can only warn or recommend.

Finding: governed continuity without structural refusal does not control Actuation.

### 101. Candidate 8.1: bonded-root qualification, delegation, and closure-failure semantics

Candidate cluster 3 already separates root identity, authority source, accountability-bearing principal, bonded-root relation, and root continuity. The stress test exposes one unresolved extension:

> How should legitimate delegation and separation of duties be represented without circular root definition, forced principal fusion, or automatic closure failure?

This candidate must pressure-test at least three representations:

1. **Composite principal:** an independently constituted organization, quorum, institution, or defined collective is the bonded root.
2. **Multiple traversals and delegation seams:** authority-holder, operator, maintainer, and service provider remain distinct roots whose traversals and authority representations compose.
3. **Authority-accountability closure failure:** neither a qualifying composite principal nor a coherent set of bonded traversals can be established.

The candidate must define:

- which principal types may qualify in the substrate-neutral core;
- what evidence constitutes a composite principal;
- how delegated authority differs from accountability termination;
- how authority to act differs from authority to authorize;
- whether one action can contain several authority-bearing roles without violating the one-traversal unit;
- and when the present term `orphaned` should be replaced or qualified as **authority-accountability closure failure**.

This candidate extends cluster 3. It does not supersede cluster 3 and is not admitted by registration.

### 102. Candidate 8.2: stated normative stance and status separation

MICRM-v0.2 chooses whole-consequence binding by default and places unresolved architectural uncertainty against an accountability exit. That is not a purely descriptive discovery. It is a normative structural-accountability choice.

A future draft must state:

- why the burden is allocated against exit;
- why opacity and under-instrumentation should not create exculpation;
- what instrumentation incentive the rule is intended to create;
- what costs, false positives, and operational burdens the choice accepts;
- and that legal liability, fault, damages, and apportionment remain overlay results.

The draft must also separate three independent findings:

```text
structural accountability status:
  bound / exited / authority-accountability closure failure / unresolved

participation or contribution finding:
  established / excluded / unresolved

legal or overlay result:
  supplied only by an attached regime
```

A failed exit must not be represented as factual proof of contribution. A structural bound state must not be represented as legal liability.

The public function of the provenance appendix must also be stated: document identity and development integrity, not normative or legal authority.

#### 109A.1 Candidate 8.1 confirmed clauses (bonded-root qualification and delegation)

The following three clauses are ratified as amended and enter the register verbatim. They sit under the first invariant and the bonded-root qualification rule.

**Composite same-principal qualification** [Normative commitment; ratified as amended]

Same-principal termination is established by terminal-principal identity at the incident resolution, not by membership overlap, shared ownership, common control, organizational affiliation, employment relationship, or a common human endpoint.

A composite principal may qualify as one bonded root only where independently supported evidence establishes the collective’s identity, decision rule, authority-bearing capacity, accountability-bearing capacity, and continuity over the relevant interval.

Where one path appears to terminate at the composite principal and the other at a constituent, same-principal termination is established only where independently supported evidence shows that the constituent endpoint is representational rather than terminal and that the relevant authority or accountability obligation remains vested in the composite principal.

Where independently supported evidence establishes that the constituent and composite are distinct terminal principals, authority-accountability closure fails. Where terminal-principal identity or representative capacity cannot be established or excluded, closure is unresolved.

An action is classified as orphaned only where different terminal principals are established. Unresolved closure is recorded separately and cannot satisfy the first invariant. Neither closure failure nor unresolved closure is repaired by shared membership, ownership, control, affiliation, employment, or a shared human endpoint.

**Human and institutional termination** [Normative commitment; ratified as amended]

A terminal bonded root may be a natural person, legal entity, institution, or defined collective that satisfies the applicable composite-principal qualification.

The MICRM core does not require that a bonded root ultimately terminate at a human principal. A legal, regulatory, contractual, safety, or sector-specific overlay may impose an ultimate-human-responsibility requirement; the core does not.

The root’s principal type, identity, authority-bearing capacity, accountability-bearing capacity, and applicable decision or representation rules must be established by independently supported evidence at the incident’s selected resolution.

Principal identity is not established solely by a technical identifier, ownership relation, product label, organizational affiliation, employment or agency relationship, or trace to a human participant. A declaration of principal type is evidence but is not sufficient by itself.

**Investigative-frame linkage for root resolution** [ratified with alternative-resolution and invariance clauses]

The resolution at which terminal-principal identity, composite-principal qualification, and authority-accountability closure are evaluated is an investigative-frame selection.

The selecting authority, authority source, chosen resolution, relevant inclusions and exclusions, and any dark surface created by that selection are recorded under investigative-frame authority governance.

Investigative-frame authority may select the resolution at which the claim is evaluated. It cannot establish principal identity by declaration, convert membership or affiliation into identity, or suppress an evidence-compatible alternative resolution.

Where two or more admissible evidence-compatible resolutions produce different bonded-root or closure findings, the resolution fork is retained and any dependent accountability exit remains unresolved. Where the result is invariant across every admissible resolution, resolution uncertainty does not block that result.

The remaining cluster-3 propositions (identity ceiling, traceability-not-bonding, bonded-root relation, authority-to-authorize separation, root-continuity, multi-root non-collapse) enter as evaluated in the cluster-3 closure record and require no further amendment.

#### 109A.3 Candidate 8.5 confirmed rule (contribution-class governance), repairs integrated

Confirmed core rule, with the four reviewer repairs integrated:

1. **Admissibility gate:** a proposed class enters the admissible evidence-compatible set only where every result-relevant field is grounded by the selected consequence, incident evidence, receiving-interface semantics, authority and provenance predicates, temporal and quantitative relations, multiplicity, persistence, composition conditions, and selected resolution.
2. **No bare-imaginability unresolvedness** [Normative commitment]: an unsupported hypothetical class does not enter the admissible set and does not create unresolvedness.
3. **Competing-class burden** [Normative commitment]: where retained admissible classes produce different MICRM outcomes, the affected finding is unresolved.
4. **Result invariance** [Normative commitment]: where every retained admissible class produces the same result, class-selection uncertainty does not block that result.
5. **Class-version revision:** A revision creates a new versioned contribution-class definition. Every prior attachment, propagation, cut, exclusion, candidate-root, dark-surface, and exit finding whose result depended on a revised field is reopened for the new class version and may not be carried forward without re-establishment. Findings independent of the revised fields persist. Prior findings remain valid only as findings under their original class version. The revision authority, changed fields, reason, evidence, affected findings, and reopening result are recorded.
6. **Admissible-set persistence:** A newly proposed admissible class joins the versioned admissible-class set. It does not supersede or remove another admissible class unless independently supported evidence establishes that the earlier class is inadmissible, no longer incident-compatible, or was replaced through an authorized revision subject to the reopening rule.
7. **Class-discovery bound:** Failure to discover another contribution class does not establish that none exists. A residual class-discovery dark surface blocks a dependent finding only where independently supported evidence identifies a bounded unexamined source, class family, interface semantic, evidence set, or resolution dimension capable of yielding an admissible outcome-changing class. Bare epistemic possibility does not create unresolvedness.

Terminology: **result-relevant fields** are fields whose value can change class admissibility or an attachment, propagation, cut, exclusion, candidate-root, dark-surface, or exit finding. This term replaces the earlier "material fields" usage, which is not adopted while Candidate 8.3 materiality remains unresolved.

The versioned contribution-class governance record (proposer, class version, predecessor version, grounding evidence per field, retained competing classes, reopened and preserved findings, result delta, discovery sources, residual class-discovery dark surface, closure finding) is retained as apparatus.
