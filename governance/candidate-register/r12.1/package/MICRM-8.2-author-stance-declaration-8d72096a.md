Candidate 8.2 author stance declaration

I affirm all four rulings identified by the closure evaluation. The distinction between the model’s output findings and the normative character of the rules producing those findings is necessary.

1. MICRM is not purely descriptive

MICRM is a normative structural-accountability reference model.

It describes machine-initiated actions, authority paths, accountability paths, traversals, Controls, Gates, Actuation, Consequences, and evidence structures. It also makes explicit normative choices about how unresolved structural and evidentiary uncertainty affects an accountability exit.

Those choices must not be presented as facts discovered from the architecture itself.

2. Burden is intentionally allocated against exit

MICRM adopts whole-consequence binding by default and requires independently supported structural evidence for an exit because:

the roots that design, authorize, operate, and instrument machine systems generally control more of the evidence needed to establish cuts and exclusions than an external investigator;
allowing missing or root-controlled evidence to support exit would reward opacity, weak instrumentation, and selective retention;
requiring evidence-supported exit creates an incentive to design systems with observable authority, control, execution, and consequence boundaries;
substrate-neutral structural binding must be determined before legal, contractual, regulatory, or economic apportionment is applied;
uncertainty about contribution must not be converted into exculpation merely because the system cannot reconstruct its own operation.

This is an adopted precautionary allocation of structural uncertainty. It is not a logical inevitability and is not claimed to be the only possible accountability policy.

3. Costs are knowingly accepted

MICRM knowingly accepts that this stance may produce:

broader presumptively bound sets than a contribution-first model;
higher instrumentation, retention, and independent-verification burdens;
fewer event-specific exits for opaque, stochastic, fused, or Gate-less systems;
unresolved structural accountability where factual contribution has not been established;
operational friction for shared infrastructure and multi-party systems;
and pressure on deployers and providers to redesign systems before they can support strong accountability-exit claims.

These are not treated as factual findings of wrongdoing.

MICRM accepts those costs because the alternative would permit opacity, irreproducibility, and missing evidence to operate as structural safe harbors.

The costs must be stated openly and evaluated through implementation profiles and overlays. They must not be hidden behind the language of neutral description.

4. Structural status, factual contribution, and legal result are separate

A future MICRM draft must distinguish:

structural accountability status:
  bound
  exited
  authority-accountability closure failure
  unresolved

participation or contribution finding:
  established
  excluded
  unresolved

legal or overlay result:
  determined only by the applicable
  legal, regulatory, contractual,
  safety, or sector-specific regime

A root may remain structurally bound because an exit is unresolved without its factual contribution being established.

A failed exit is not proof of causation, misconduct, negligence, fault, or legal liability.

A contribution finding does not determine the share of responsibility.

A legal or overlay result does not retroactively change the MICRM structural evidence finding.

5. Normative commitments must be marked as commitments

The core text will not rely solely on a prefatory disclaimer while retaining inevitability language inside the mechanics.

Future draft text must identify its claim class explicitly. At minimum:

Structural definition
Normative commitment
Evidence rule
Assumption
Open construction question
Overlay-dependent result

The second invariant and every rule allocating unresolved uncertainty against exit must be presented as Normative commitments, with their rationale and accepted costs stated near the operative rule.

The model may still use direct normative language such as “a root is bound” or “an unresolved path prevents exit,” but the reader must be told that these are consequences of an adopted MICRM commitment, not facts compelled by the existence of a run graph.

This resolves the evaluator’s concern that an abstract acknowledgment in the introduction could become “candor theater” while the mechanics continue presenting policy as inevitability.

6. Provenance does not establish correctness or authority

MICRM’s provenance apparatus establishes:

document identity;
development chronology;
exact source and revision state;
author and reviewer dispositions;
and resistance to silent modification.

It does not establish:

architectural correctness;
empirical validation;
legal authority;
regulatory acceptance;
or normative force outside the commitments expressly adopted by the model.
