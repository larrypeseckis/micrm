## 1. Author dispositions

### 65.2 Claim-bounded verification

**Disposition: Superseded as a standalone proposition.**

The permission half is derivable from canonical MICRM-v0.2's mechanism-neutral evidence standard. The non-derivable content survives in a successor candidate covering restricted-evidence admissibility:

- investigative-frame authority over claim selection;
- claim-object binding, with execution binding required only for runtime claims;
- disclosure of verified scope, dependencies, limitations, and residual dark surface;
- sufficient independence of the verification arrangement for the named error class;
- resistance to narrow-claim laundering.

### 65.7 Alternative-verification ceiling

**Disposition: Superseded as a standalone proposition.**

The single-mechanism ceiling is derivable from exact-claims discipline and the restricted-evidence successor. The surviving non-derivable content moves to a separate successor candidate covering verification scope and composition:

- findings do not extend beyond verified scope;
- causal explanation is excluded unless independently verified;
- multiple narrow verifications compose only where their scope union and seams are evidenced to cover the claimed scope;
- scope validity is bounded by its recorded validity interval, with cross-reference to Gate-to-Actuation validity continuity where relevant.

### Successor structure

Do not combine every surviving clause into one long proposition. Register two successor candidates:

1. **Restricted-evidence admissibility**
   - defines when restricted evidence may satisfy a MICRM evidentiary obligation;
   - contains the 65.1 confidentiality guard and the 65.10 burden result;
   - links to investigative-frame authority and claim-object binding.

2. **Verification scope and composition ceiling**
   - limits findings to verified scope;
   - governs composition of multiple scoped verifications;
   - excludes causal explanation unless separately verified;
   - records validity intervals and scope seams.

Execution binding remains a separate candidate under 65.3.

## 2. Closure evaluation: 65.10 Confidentiality cannot exculpate

### Candidate text

> Where the evidence required for an exit cannot be made available through direct or alternative verification, the exit remains unestablished. The result is continued binding by burden, not a factual finding of wrongdoing.

### Criterion findings

**4.1 Provenance and identity:** Established. Cluster 6, r8 origin, unchanged through r10.

**4.2 Gap necessity:** Fails as an independent core proposition. Canonical MICRM-v0.2 already provides the result: an exit is available only where its evidentiary conditions are established; unresolved evidence blocks exit without proving contribution or wrongdoing. Confidentiality is one possible reason evidence remains unavailable or unresolved, not a separate accountability rule.

**4.3 Exact-claim sufficiency:** The phrase "cannot be made available" is too narrow. Evidence may be available but insufficient, outside verified scope, unbound to the claim object, dependent for the named error class, stale, or otherwise unresolved. If retained as guidance or successor wording, use:

> Where evidence required for a claim or accountability exit is unavailable, insufficient, unbound, outside verified scope, or unresolved under direct or alternative verification, the claim or exit remains unestablished. Continued binding by burden is not a factual finding of wrongdoing.

**4.4 Non-redundancy:** The accountability result is fully derivable from canonical v0.2 and the 65.1 guard. Its useful function is explanatory: it prevents proprietary or restricted status from being misread as exculpation.

**4.5 Scope classification:** Derivative guidance and a burden-result clause inside the Restricted-evidence admissibility successor. Not a separate core proposition.

**4.6 Substrate treatment:** Passes as derivative guidance across proprietary AI systems, classified evidence, privileged institutional evidence, vendor-controlled cloud systems, and restricted OT records.

**4.7 Operational findings:**
- required evidence established: the claim or exit may proceed subject to all other conditions;
- required evidence unavailable, insufficient, unbound, outside scope, or unresolved: claim or exit unestablished;
- no wrongdoing finding follows solely from the evidentiary failure.

**4.8 Evidence and dark-surface treatment:** Consistent with v0.2. Restricted access may be represented through direct or alternative verification, but any unresolved residual dark surface continues to limit the claim.

**4.9 Adversarial pressure:**
1. confidentiality-as-exemption: blocked by the ordinary burden rule;
2. nominal disclosure of irrelevant evidence: blocked by claim-object and verified-scope requirements;
3. dependent verification presented as independent: blocked by the named-error-class dependence profile;
4. restricted status used to prevent challenge: preserved as unresolved evidence, not converted into exculpation.

**4.10 Interaction consistency:** Consistent with 65.1, the 65.2 successor, 65.7's scope ceiling, 65.3 execution binding, and investigative-frame authority.

**4.11 Scope-authority ownership:** No new scope expansion. No additional author scope ruling required.

**4.12 Open questions affected:** Confirms that restricted evidence changes verification method and disclosure route, not the ordinary evidentiary burden or accountability result.
